COAT Paint in the Real World: A West London Bedroom Repaint

Every so often a job doubles as a proper product test. This bedroom repaint was one of those — a soft, dusty-pink scheme from COAT, paired with a hand-applied mural feature wall installed by a specialist third-party decorator. It gave us a good chance to see how COAT performs from tin to top coat, on a real site, under real time pressure.

In this guide

  1. The setup
  2. Application and drying time
  3. Coverage
  4. Conclusion
  5. FAQs

The Setup

The brief was simple: a warm, muted pink across the walls and ceiling, with one wall reserved for a large painted mural — a soft, cloud-and-horizon scene applied by another tradesperson once our base coats were down. Our job was to prep the room, cut in and roll the COAT colour on the remaining three walls and the ceiling, and hand back a clean, even finish ready for the mural artist to work from.

Kit for the job was kept deliberately simple:

  • COAT emulsion (their standard 2.5L tin)
  • Purdy Pro-Extra Altitude roller sleeve, 10mm pile, 229mm — good for a smooth-to-semi-smooth wall like this one
  • Standard roller tray and frame
  • Masking tape and dust sheets for a clean cut-in along the skirting and architrave

Application and Drying Time

We applied three coats of COAT across the walls to get full, even depth of colour — this particular pink is a mid-tone that showed a touch of patchiness after coat one, as most colours in this depth range do over a previously painted surface. By coat two the colour was largely true, and the third coat evened out any roller marks and gave a consistent sheen from every angle.

Drying time between coats was genuinely useful on site: touch-dry and ready to recoat in around 1 hour. On a job with a mural artist waiting to start on the feature wall, that turnaround mattered — it meant we weren't losing half a day between coats, and the room was fully finished and dry well within a single working day.

Coverage

This is where COAT stood out. For a room of this size — walls and ceiling, minus the mural wall — we used under 5 litres across three coats. That's strong coverage for a three-coat application on a colour this saturated, and it kept material costs sensible without any compromise on the final finish.

⚠️ Wallpaper mural installation on the feature wall was completed by a third-party specialist decorator, not EveroDecor.

Conclusion

COAT performed well on every measure that matters on a working site: colour consistency after three coats, a genuinely quick 1-hour recoat window, and coverage that stretched further than we expected for the depth of colour used. Paired with a well-chosen Purdy Pro-Extra roller, the finish was smooth, even, and ready to sit alongside the mural feature wall without any awkward transition. For anyone weighing up COAT for a similar project, this one's a clear thumbs up on coverage and dry time in particular.
