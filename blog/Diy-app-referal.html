DIY, Apps or a Recommended Painter? How to Choose a Painter & Decorator

When you need to redecorate your home, there are usually three choices: do the work yourself, find a painter through an online platform, or hire someone recommended by a friend. Each option can work — but each comes with different costs, risks and practical considerations.

Three ways to get your home painted

DIY

You control the timing and can save on labour, but you take responsibility for preparation, materials, equipment, application and finishing.

Online platforms

Apps as TaskRabbit, Checkatrade, Airtasker, mybuilder can make it easier to compare traders and reviews, but a profile or verification badge does not automatically mean that a trader is right for your particular project.

Friend's recommendation

A personal recommendation can be valuable, but it is still worth checking the person's previous work, quotation, insurance and experience.

1. DIY: It Can Look Easier on YouTube Than It Really Is

There is an enormous amount of painting advice online. You can find videos showing how to prepare walls, cut in around windows, paint ceilings, repair cracks and achieve a smooth finish.

This information can be very useful. The problem is that a short video can make the process look much easier than it is in a real room.

DIY painting involves more than applying the final coat of paint.

Professional-looking painting often depends heavily on preparation rather than simply putting paint on a wall.

You may need to:

  • Remove loose or damaged paint
  • Fill holes and cracks
  • Sand uneven surfaces
  • Repair small imperfections
  • Protect floors, furniture and fittings
  • Deal with previous poor paintwork
  • Prepare woodwork
  • Apply primer where necessary
  • Choose the correct paint and finish
  • Cut in accurately around ceilings, doors and windows
  • Apply the correct number of coats
  • Allow sufficient drying time

The cheapest option is not always the option that costs the least. A poor finish can mean buying more paint, spending additional hours correcting mistakes or eventually paying someone else to put the work right.

2. Hiring Through an App or Online Platform

Online platforms can make it easier to find tradespeople. You can usually compare profiles, read reviews and contact several people about your project.

That convenience is useful, but a profile on an app does not automatically mean that someone is the right painter for your particular job.

Read the reviews — not just the star rating

Look for comments about:

  • Preparation
  • Quality of the finish
  • Reliability
  • Communication
  • Tidiness
  • Completing the job
  • Whether the customer would hire the person again

A large number of reviews can be useful, but consider whether the reviews relate to work similar to yours.

What does “vetted” actually mean?

Different platforms use different forms of verification. If a platform says that a trader is “vetted”, “verified” or “checked”, find out what that actually means.

It may refer to identity verification, references, insurance, qualifications, background checks or another form of screening. These checks do not necessarily tell you how well the person paints.

A trader can be verified by a platform and still not necessarily be the right painter for a particular project.

Look at the trader's own work

Ask for photographs of completed projects, particularly work similar to yours. If you need an entire flat redecorated, photographs of one small feature wall may not tell you much about the person's ability to manage a larger project.

3. A Recommendation From a Friend Isn't Always a Guarantee

Personal recommendations can be excellent. If someone you trust has recently had a great experience with a painter, that can be a valuable starting point.

But was the job actually similar to yours?

A recommendation is useful, but the project and the trader's experience still need to be checked.

A painter who did an excellent job decorating one bedroom may not necessarily have experience with a larger project involving several rooms, extensive preparation, damaged walls or detailed woodwork.

There is also a practical problem with recommendations: people can sometimes rely heavily on the trust behind the introduction and do fewer checks than they would with a stranger.

“He's a friend of my brother and he does painting.”

That is not necessarily the same as having a documented history of professional work.

There are real situations where homeowners have hired someone through a friend's recommendation and later experienced poor-quality work or an unfinished project. These examples do not mean that personal recommendations are unreliable. They show why the recommendation should be a starting point for checking someone, not the only check you make.

4. What Should You Check Before Hiring a Painter?

Regardless of how you found the person, consider asking the same basic questions.

Before the first brush touches the wall

  1. Can they show you previous work? Look for projects similar to yours.
  2. What preparation is included? Painting and proper surface preparation are not necessarily the same thing.
  3. What materials will be used? Ask about the paint, finish and whether materials are included.
  4. Do they have appropriate insurance? Ask what cover they carry for the work.
  5. Can they provide a clear written quotation? This helps avoid misunderstandings.
  6. How will unexpected problems be handled? Discuss additional preparation or repairs before work starts.
  7. How will payments work? Understand exactly what you are paying for before making substantial payments.

Understand what the quotation includes

  • Areas to be painted
  • Surface preparation
  • Ceilings and woodwork
  • Paint brand and finish
  • Who buys the materials
  • Expected number of coats
  • Repairs and additional preparation
  • Estimated duration
  • Total price or pricing method

5. How Do Professional Painters and Decorators Charge?

One of the questions homeowners often have is whether a professional painter charges by the hour, by the day, by the room or for the whole project.

There isn't one universal method. Many professional decorators quote for the particular work involved, while some platforms present traders' prices as an hourly rate.

Pricing method What to consider
Hourly Easy to understand, but the final cost can be harder to predict if the amount of preparation or the duration of the work changes.
Daily rate Useful for projects where the amount of work is reasonably clear, but the total cost still depends on how many days are required.
Per room Can make budgeting easier when the scope of the room is clearly defined. Check exactly what preparation and materials are included.
Fixed project quotation Can make the overall budget clearer when the scope of work has been properly inspected and agreed.

The important question is not simply “What is the hourly rate?” It is: “What will the total project cost, and what exactly is included?”

A low initial price can become more expensive if important preparation, repairs, materials or additional work were not included in the original quotation.

6. Don't Choose a Painter Based Only on the Price

Price obviously matters. But painting is one of those jobs where the cheapest quotation isn't necessarily the cheapest overall outcome.

Consider the full picture:

Price + preparation + materials + workmanship + reliability + time + communication.

Someone charging less may be perfectly capable and offer excellent value. Someone charging more may also provide excellent work. The important thing is understanding what you are actually getting for the price.

7. So, Should You DIY or Hire a Professional?

A better question than “DIY or professional?” is: “What is the most sensible way to achieve the finish I want within my time and budget?”

DIY can make sense when:

  • You enjoy DIY
  • You have enough time
  • The surfaces are already in good condition
  • The project is relatively straightforward
  • You are comfortable doing preparation and finishing work

Hiring a professional can make more sense when:

  • You have several rooms to complete
  • The walls need significant preparation
  • You need a consistent finish
  • You have limited time
  • You don't have the equipment
  • You want someone experienced to deal with unexpected problems

And if you decide to hire someone, how you found them is less important than how thoroughly you check them.

A Final Thought: Painting Is More Than Putting Paint on a Wall

The final result can depend heavily on what happens before the paint goes on. You can find excellent tradespeople through online platforms. You can find excellent tradespeople through personal recommendations. And you can certainly achieve good results yourself.

But none of these routes removes the need to do your own checks.

If a YouTube tutorial makes a painting project look incredibly easy, remember that you're watching the finished demonstration — not necessarily the hours of preparation, problem-solving and cleaning that happen around it.

Take your time choosing the approach, and don't be afraid to ask questions before the first brush touches the wall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to paint a room myself?

DIY can reduce labour costs, but you should also consider paint, preparation materials, tools, equipment and your own time. A mistake can also create additional costs.

Are painters found through apps safe to hire?

Online platforms can be useful, but you should still check reviews, previous work, insurance, the scope of the quotation and what a platform's “vetted” or “verified” status actually means.

Is a friend's recommendation enough?

A recommendation is a useful starting point, but it is still sensible to check the painter's previous work, experience with similar projects, insurance and written quotation.

Do painters charge by the hour or by the job?

Both approaches exist. Some traders and online platforms use hourly or daily rates, while many decorators quote for the particular project. Always ask what is included and what the expected total cost is.

Why is preparation so important?

Filling, sanding, repairing, priming and protecting surrounding areas can have a major effect on the final appearance and durability of the finished paintwork.
