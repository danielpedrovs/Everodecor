WHY PRIMING MATTERS

Primer is not an optional extra on every surface

Primer helps paint stick to the surface, seals porous areas so the topcoat dries evenly, and can block stains from showing through. On some surfaces it is essential; on others it can be skipped.

Knowing which is which depends on the surface, its condition and the paint going on top. Using the wrong product, or none at all, can lead to patchy colour, uneven sheen or paint that peels.

At EveroDecor, priming and finishing are chosen to suit the surface, not applied by default.

PRIMERS & FINISHES

Choosing the right primer and finish

Primers are made for different jobs. A general-purpose primer suits many bare surfaces, a stain-blocking primer is intended for marks that might otherwise show through, and a bonding primer is designed for smoother or glossy surfaces that paint does not grip easily.

Finishes vary too. Matt is commonly used on walls and ceilings, while satin, eggshell or gloss are more often chosen for woodwork and areas that need to stand up to cleaning and wear. Higher-sheen finishes tend to show surface flaws more, which is another reason preparation matters.

The right combination depends on the surface, the room and the result you want. We can discuss the options before work begins.

COMMON FINISH PROBLEMS

Is your paint finish letting you down?

Patchy or uneven sheen

Repairs and bare patches that were not primed can absorb paint differently, leaving dull or shiny patches in the finished wall.

Peeling or poor adhesion

Paint applied over glossy, dusty or unprepared surfaces can fail to bond. Cleaning, keying the surface and a suitable primer all help.

Stains bleeding through

Some marks can show through ordinary paint coat after coat. A stain-blocking primer is intended for this, though the cause of the stain should be looked at too.

New or bare plaster

Fresh plaster is very absorbent and usually needs specific preparation before decorative coats, following the paint manufacturer's guidance.

FAQ

Priming and finishing questions

Do I always need to prime before painting?

Not always. Bare plaster, filler, bare wood, stains and glossy surfaces generally benefit from priming, while sound, previously painted walls in a similar colour often do not need it. It depends on the surface.

Can paint with primer in it replace a separate primer?

Paints that claim to include primer can help on some surfaces, but bare, stained or problem areas often still need a dedicated primer. We can advise on which suits your surface.

Which finish should I use for woodwork?

Woodwork is usually painted in a harder-wearing finish such as satin, eggshell or gloss, because it is cleaned and touched more than walls. The choice depends on the look you want and the condition of the wood.

Can priming be part of a wider painting project?

Yes. Priming and finishing normally form part of interior decorating, alongside filling, sanding and painting walls, ceilings and woodwork.
