Valspar Trade vs Dulux Trade: Which Vinyl Matt Gives the Better Finish?

Valspar Trade and Dulux Trade are two popular water-based vinyl matt paints used by decorators across the UK. This guide compares their coverage, recoat times and wet-edge performance, while looking at what really causes patchy or uneven walls.

If you paint walls and ceilings regularly in London, you will probably end up comparing Valspar Trade and Dulux Trade at some point. Both are professional water-based vinyl matt paints and both are widely available through trade suppliers.

The real differences are often found in the details: how much surface you can cover with a litre, how long you need to wait before applying the next coat, and how well the paint keeps a wet edge while you work. These factors can make a noticeable difference when you are trying to achieve a smooth, even finish without patching or flashing.

Coverage: Close, but Dulux Has a Slight Edge

Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt is listed at 16m² per litre, while Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt is listed at up to 17m² per litre. In most situations, you should expect to apply two full coats to achieve an even and consistent finish.

Product Listed coverage Recoat time
Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt 16m² per litre 2 – 4 hours
Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt Up to 17m² per litre 2 – 4 hours

On paper, that is only around a 6% difference in coverage. In a larger property, however, that small difference can add up and may mean using an additional tin, or part of one. There are also two important points to consider:

  • “Up to” coverage is a best-case figure. It generally assumes a sound, smooth and evenly absorbent surface. Fresh plaster, porous walls and repaired areas can require considerably more paint.
  • Valspar has different products within its range. For example, Valspar Tough Matt is listed at 17m² per litre, so the lower coverage figure for Vinyl Matt should not be applied to every Valspar Trade product.

Waiting Time Between Coats: Similar on Paper

Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt is listed with a recoat time of 2–4 hours, while Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt is also generally listed at around 2–4 hours. Some retailers give longer drying times for particular Dulux products or colours, so it is always worth checking the technical information on the specific tin you are using.

In real decorating conditions, these times should be treated as a guide rather than a guarantee. Cold temperatures, high humidity and poor ventilation can all slow the drying process. These conditions are particularly common in older London properties.

If paint is taking considerably longer to dry than expected, the conditions in the room are often more important than the brand of paint. Giving the first coat enough time to dry properly can make the second coat easier to apply and help prevent dragging, roller marks and uneven texture.

Where Patchiness Comes From

When a painted wall looks patchy or uneven, the paint's coverage figure is rarely the only factor. In many cases, the underlying surface and application technique have a much greater influence.

The most common causes include:

  1. Uneven absorption. Filled cracks, repaired plaster and bare areas absorb paint differently from the surrounding surface. This can create flashing, where repaired areas remain visible after painting. A suitable mist coat or primer can help control absorption before applying the finishing coats.
  2. Losing the wet edge. If the paint begins to dry before you blend into the next section, you can create visible lap marks. Working systematically and maintaining a wet edge helps prevent this.
  3. Recoating too soon. Applying another coat before the first one has properly dried can cause the roller to drag the paint underneath, creating texture and unevenness.
  4. Inconsistent roller loading or pressure. Using too little or too much paint, or changing the pressure applied to the roller, can create differences in film thickness that become more noticeable under strong or angled light.

One of Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt's useful characteristics is its extended wet-edge time. This gives decorators more time to blend adjacent sections before the paint starts to dry. The product is also relatively thick compared with some other paints in the Valspar range, which means decorators may choose to thin it for certain applications.

That can be useful when preparing a mist coat, but applying a thick paint without the right technique can also increase the visibility of roller texture. As always, the surface, roller, paint loading and application method all play a role.

Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt has been a long-established choice among professional decorators and is widely known for its ease of application and predictable finish. For many decorators, familiarity is a major advantage because they already know how the product behaves in different conditions.

Practical Tips for a Consistent Finish

  • Prepare the surface properly before painting. Mist coat new or repaired plaster and spot-prime areas where necessary.
  • Work in manageable sections and maintain a wet edge so that each section blends into the next.
  • Do not rush the second coat. Follow the recommended recoat time and allow additional drying time in cold, damp or poorly ventilated rooms.
  • Use the same paint product and, where possible, the same batch throughout a wall or room. For larger areas, mixing tins together can help maintain colour consistency.
  • Use suitable rollers and keep the roller consistently loaded. Avoid excessive pressure, particularly towards the end of each pass.
  • Check the finished surface in natural daylight and, where possible, under angled or raking light before deciding whether another coat is needed.

Good preparation usually matters more than the paint brand. Even a premium trade paint can produce a poor finish if the surface has not been properly prepared or the paint is applied incorrectly.

The Verdict

Based on the published specifications, Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt has a small advantage in listed coverage, while the recoat times are broadly similar. Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt has the advantage of an extended wet edge, which can be helpful when working on larger walls or when conditions make the paint more difficult to blend.

However, the difference between the two paints is unlikely to be the deciding factor in whether a wall looks professional. Preparation, surface condition, drying conditions and application technique will usually have a much greater impact on the final result.

If you are choosing between Valspar Trade and Dulux Trade, consider the product that suits the surface, the conditions and your own working method. A paint you know well and can apply consistently will often produce a better result than simply choosing the brand with the highest specification on the tin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Valspar Trade cover as well as Dulux Trade?

Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt is listed at 16m² per litre, while Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt is listed at up to 17m² per litre. The difference is relatively small, and actual coverage depends heavily on the condition, texture and absorbency of the surface.

Is the drying time longer for Valspar Trade?

Both products are generally listed with a 2–4 hour recoat time. Cold, damp or poorly ventilated rooms can slow either paint, so always check the manufacturer's guidance for the specific product and allow additional drying time when conditions are poor.

How do I avoid patchy walls with either paint?

Start with proper surface preparation, control absorption on repaired areas, maintain a wet edge, load the roller consistently and allow the first coat to dry fully before applying the second.

Which is better for professional decorators: Valspar Trade or Dulux Trade?

Both can produce a good professional finish when applied correctly. Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt has a long-established reputation and predictable application, while Valspar Trade Vinyl Matt offers useful wet-edge characteristics. The best choice depends on the surface, conditions and the decorator's preferred product.
